## Purpose
Choices you make as an undergraduate student impact your opportunities and outcomes in career post-graduation. This document compiles best practices for UW Bothell students who want to increase their technical/professional toolset before graduation.

## Courses and Electives

### Choosing Electives
Choosing electives is important as they can help narrow down what field you may want to work in post-grad. It may also help to look at job postings in your target field and pick electives that cover the skills they list. Check your picks with your academic advisor before registering so they fit your degree plan.

### Learning Tools Taught in Class
The tools you learn how to use in class are important, as they will most likely show up later on in your career. Or they can be good to know of in case you are asked about them in an interview. Keep track of the software, equipment, and languages you us in your courses so you can pull from it when you build your resume. ## Extracurricular Experience

### Undergraduate Research
Undergraduate research is a way to work with professional staff to get hands-on experience in project-based environments. Email professors whose work lines up with your interests and ask about openings. Research also gives you mentors who can write recommendation letters later.

### Personal Projects
Completing personal projects gets you experience at your own pace. It's also a great way to tinker with new tools, and learn new skills, as you don't have a grade or deadline hanging over you. Pick a small project that builds a skill you want, and document it with photos and a short write-up so you have something to show recruiters.

## Technical Experience

### Finding Internships
Getting an internship related to the job field you want to go into post-grad, is a great way to gain both professional and technical skills. Since you are interning in a professional environment, you will learn how to navigate those settings. Technical internships are also great gateways to build upon the fundamentals that you learn in your coursework. Start searching early on sites like LinkedIn and Husky Handshake, since companies post openings months ahead. Apply to a lot of them, because rejections are normal.

## Success Strategies

### Resume Writing
It's important to tailor your resume to sell you as an applicant as best as possible. When applying in a harsh/competitive job field, it's important to stand out. Some strategies you can use are matching the keywords in the job posting, putting your most relevant projects first, and using numbers to show results where you can.

### Networking and Career Services
Gaining connections in the industry is very important. Your connections can give you an edge when you are applying to internships and jobs. Reach out to alumni and people in your clubs on LinkedIn, and ask them questions about their work rather than asking for a job.

Take advantage of the resources you have as an undergraduate student. One of those resources is the Career Services Centers. Book a resume review and a mock interview with them before you start applying.
